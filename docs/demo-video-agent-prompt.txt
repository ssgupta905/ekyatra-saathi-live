HOW TO USE THIS FILE
--------------------
1. Upload the source film as the input video:
       EKYATRA-Saathi-end-to-end.mp4   (12:15, 1920x1080, in this same folder)
2. Paste everything below the line marked "PROMPT BEGINS" into the agent's
   prompt box (HeyGen, Synthesia, Argil, Creatify — this is written to be
   tool-agnostic).

Two things to know before you do.

* USE THE SOURCE AUDIO. The film already carries two voices that matter: an
  English narration, and the Hindi that the family's phone itself speaks. The
  Hindi is not decoration — it is the product working — and it must survive
  into the final cut. A generator that mutes the source and replaces everything
  with an avatar track will delete the single most persuasive thing in the
  recording. Specific instructions for this are in section 4.

* THE BRIEF SAYS "NOT MERELY A SCREEN RECORDING." That is the bar this prompt
  is written against: a presenter who introduces the team, explains how the
  problem was approached, credits the doctor and technology partners, and then
  shows the working product. Section 2 is therefore as important as section 3.

================================ PROMPT BEGINS =================================

ROLE
----
You are producing a recorded, narrated DEMO WALKTHROUGH for EKYATRA Saathi, an
entry in Health-a-thon 2026 by team Bits2026. You are given a 12-minute
narrated screen recording of the working product. Turn it into a tighter,
presenter-led film that a judging panel will watch end to end.

You are re-cutting and re-presenting real footage. Do not generate product
screens, invent numbers, fabricate logos, or add testimonials.

AUDIENCE
--------
Clinicians, health-system people and technical judges watching many
submissions in one sitting. They decide in the first forty seconds whether to
keep watching.

The one idea they must leave with:
  A family travelling 600 km for cancer care is told whether today's trip is
  worth making — and the thing that decides is not a chatbot.

OUTPUT
------
  Length     5:30 to 6:30
  Aspect     16:9, 1920x1080, H.264
  Presenter  One avatar. Indian English, warm and plain-spoken — a colleague
             explaining their work, not a product launch. Full frame for
             sections 1, 2 and 6; picture-in-picture lower-left over the screen
             recording for the rest.
  Captions   Burned-in English subtitles throughout. Many judges watch muted.
  Pace       Let the recording breathe. When a verdict changes on screen, stop
             talking and let it change.

Also export, if supported:
  - a clean version with no burned-in captions
  - a 60-second vertical (9:16) cut, built from section 1 and the Wait-to-Come
    moment at 10:18-10:48
  - the final narration script as a text file, so it can be checked line by
    line against the ACCURACY section below

1. OPEN — THE PROBLEM                                            0:00 - 0:45
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Presenter full frame for the first two sentences, then over footage from
00:09-00:28 and 02:49-03:06.

  "Sixty per cent of the patients at India's biggest cancer hospital come from
   outside the state. Most travel more than five hundred kilometres. They
   arrive, and the report isn't back — so they go home, and they come again.
   Eighty-three per cent of those families hit catastrophic non-medical costs.
   More than half borrow or sell something to cope.
   Saathi asks one question, every day, for every family: is this trip worth
   making?"

2. THE TEAM AND HOW WE WORKED                                    0:45 - 1:40
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Presenter full frame. Build a simple name-and-role card for each person as they
are introduced — no stock photos of people, no invented headshots. If the tool
requires an image, use a plain typographic card.

  "We are Bits2026. Five people, working as an independent team.

   Siddhartha Sengupta, our technical architect, designed the decision core and
   the rules the system is not allowed to break. Ankshuk Ray, a full-stack and
   AI engineer, built the two web products, the Android app, and the language
   and speech layer. Abhik Sengupta, a PhD scholar in the biological sciences,
   did the evidence review this is built against. Srinivas Pavan managed
   delivery across the four weeks and made the scope calls that kept it
   shippable.

   And our doctor partner, Dr Arani Chakraborty — MBBS, formerly a resident in
   neurosurgery at AIIMS Kalyani, now with the department of neurology at
   Manipal Hospital, EM Bypass.

   His contribution changed the shape of the product. He mapped the sequence a
   patient actually moves through — presentation, evaluation, the wait for a
   result, follow-up — and told us where it breaks: the points at which a
   patient is sent away and does not come back. That is why Saathi models a
   journey as a chain with a blocking step, rather than a calendar of
   appointments, and why it refuses to give a date until that chain is clear.

   We should be precise about the limits of that. It is clinical process
   review, not a clinical trial. No real patient data has been used anywhere in
   this build.

   On the technology side we built on an agentic AI layer for language, Sarvam
   for Indian language speech, and the ABDM consent model for how data is
   asked for. We
   started from a reminder app. Within a week we realised the useful question
   was the opposite one — should they come at all — and rebuilt around it."

3. THE WORKING SOLUTION                                          1:40 - 4:30
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Presenter in picture-in-picture over the footage.

3a. The navigator's board                      use 02:49-03:30 and 04:30-05:30
  "This is a hospital navigator's board: every family against every party
   holding them up — the lab, the insurer, the pharmacy, the cab, the hotel.
   Watch Meena. Her cab firm answers for itself, and the moment the last party
   is ready, Wait becomes Come and a date appears. The date is a consequence of
   readiness. It is never a guess."

3b. When something fails                                   use 03:30-04:30
  "Ravi's cab firm says it cannot. The journey stops. No date is promised,
   because there is nothing to promise — and a person, not the system, chooses
   who to ask next."

3c. The vendor's own screen                                use 06:01-06:22
  "Everyone the journey leans on gets a screen. The cab firm sees only its own
   jobs — a first name, a district, a day — and a phone number only if the
   family agreed to share it."

3d. The family's phone                            use 07:13-07:28, 10:18-10:48
  "The family has none of that complexity. One word, one date, what to bring,
   in their own language. And when the lab four hundred kilometres away
   releases the report, nobody touches the phone. Wait becomes Come, and the
   list of papers to carry appears with it."
  HOLD on the Hindi screen flipping. Do not cut during the flip.

3e. Affordability — the differentiator                     use 07:31-08:35
  "Not every family can absorb an app cab and a hotel. So Saathi asks — and
   every option is a real organisation from the hospital's own directory: a
   railway patient-quota berth, a shared jeep, a taxi. This family's ration
   card is AAY, so the concession is offered first. There is no price on the
   screen, because the system has none to give. And last on every list,
   deliberately not one of the things to buy: I cannot manage any of these —
   which doesn't book a cheaper cab. It raises a social worker."

5. WHY IT IS SAFE TO PUT IN FRONT OF A FAMILY                    5:00 - 5:40
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use 11:38-11:58.

  "Three things make this safe. The model never decides whether a family
   travels — a deterministic planner decides, and the model only puts that
   decision into their language. No report content ever enters the system;
   there is no field that could hold a diagnosis. And every decision sits in a
   hash-chained log that cannot be quietly edited. When the system does not
   know, it says Uncertain and tells a person, instead of guessing."

   Thank you."

STYLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Verbs over adjectives. "The cab firm answers for itself" beats "a powerful
  AI-driven orchestration layer."
* NO stock footage of hospitals, doctors, patients or families. The product
  footage is the proof; stock imagery makes it look like there is nothing to
  show.
* Keep the Hindi on screen untranslated, with the presenter saying what it
  means. Seeing the family's own language is the point; subtitling it away
  removes it.
* Music sparse and low, and out entirely under the verdict changes and under
  the Hindi in section 4.
* No countdown timers, swooshes or neon. The product is a calm, light
  interface. Match it.
* Do not speed-ramp the screen recording. A verdict changing at real speed is
  the evidence.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ACCURACY — DO NOT DEVIATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything below was measured or cited. Do not round it, dramatise it, or
extend it.

SAY EXACTLY:
  - "60% of patients at Tata Memorial Hospital come from outside Maharashtra"
  - "83% faced catastrophic non-medical costs" (mean Rs 88,433)
  - "53% of those travelling over 500 km borrowed or sold assets"
  - "On fifty synthetic families with planted ground truth, the hospital's
     default policy would have produced thirty wasted trips. Saathi produced
     none."
  - "No real patient data has been used."

NEVER SAY:
  - Any claim of clinical validation, hospital deployment, patient outcomes,
    or lives saved.
  - "Diagnoses", "detects", "predicts cancer", or anything implying the system
    reads results. It reads STATUS — ready or not ready — never content.
  - A number that is not in this prompt. If a figure is needed and is not
    here, drop the sentence.
  - That the AI makes the travel decision. It renders a decision the planner
    already made.

ALSO:
  - Dr Chakraborty's role is clinical process review. Do not describe him as
    having validated, trialled, approved or endorsed the product, and do not
    place him at a hospital that is deploying it.
  - Do not add a company name, funding claim, user count or partner logo.
    There are none.
  - Do not generate an avatar that is presented as a doctor or a patient.
  - One section of the source (07:31-08:35, affordability) was recorded against
    a local build rather than the live deployment, because that feature is not
    on the deployed service yet. Do not claim otherwise. Everything else in the
    film is the live deployment.
  - The team has five members. Name all five. Do not invent a sixth.
