**To:** ICMR – National Centre for Disease Informatics and Research (NCDIR), National Cancer
Registry Programme (NCRP)
**From:** Ankshuk Ray
**Subject:** Inquiry — potential validation study using cancer-care-logistics software (Saathi)

Dear NCDIR / NCRP team,

My name is Ankshuk Ray. I'm writing as an independent developer, working on a hackathon
project called **Saathi** — software that helps cancer patients and their families time their
hospital visits around when a required report, scan, or appointment is actually ready, rather
than returning on a fixed date that may turn out to be a wasted trip. The motivation comes
directly from published work on the scale of this problem at Tata Memorial Hospital: a large
share of its patients travel from outside Maharashtra, and a large share of patients studied
for certain cancers travel over 500 km for care, so an avoidable "the report isn't ready yet"
return trip is a real and measurable cost to patients and families.

Today Saathi runs entirely on synthetic, scored test data — no real patient data has been used
anywhere. Before any real-data pilot, I want to understand what it would take to validate the
system properly against real outcomes, which is where NCRP's work is directly relevant:

1. Is there an existing pathway for a non-clinical, non-diagnostic logistics tool like this to
   be evaluated using NCRP hospital-based cancer registry (HBCR) data, in partnership with a
   specific registered hospital?
2. What would NCDIR need to see before considering such a validation study — e.g. a named
   hospital partner, an Institutional Ethics Committee approval, a specific data-sharing
   request format?
3. Is there a standard data-sharing policy document I should read before approaching a
   specific HBCR-registered hospital about this?

I want to be upfront that this is an early-stage, pre-partnership inquiry — I don't yet have a
named hospital partner or an IEC approval in hand, and I'm not asking for registry data at this
stage. I'm trying to understand the right order of operations so that if a hospital partner
agrees to participate, I approach the registry process correctly rather than asking for
something out of scope.

Thank you for your time, and I'm happy to share more detail about the project (including a
short write-up / demo) if useful.

Best regards,
Ankshuk Ray
